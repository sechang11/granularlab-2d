Diameters are drawn uniformly from d(1±x). x = 0 gives a monodisperse packing, which crystallises readily; 10–20% suppresses it.

Units. Everything is SI inside (m, kg, N); only the display converts. Grain size reads in mm, the box in cm, forces in N/kN — what a granular experimentalist would actually write down. Note the disks are unit-thickness, so a force here is really force per metre of depth (N/m) and a mass is kg/m; the ratios that matter (bottom/W, K) are dimensionless either way. 30 mm grains are gravel — real bead experiments run 1–3 mm, and the slider now reaches 2 mm.

ζ is a damping ratio: γ = 2ζ√(meffk). ζ = 0 is perfectly elastic, ζ = 1 critically damped. b is background drag on every particle, independent of contact.

kn must grow with bed depth. Basal contact force rises with the weight above it, and overlap is force/kn — so a setting that gives 1% overlap at N = 800 gives 7% at N = 8000. Watch the max overlap readout: green under 1%, red over 5%. Above ~2% the packing fraction is inflated by interpenetration and Z drifts past its isostatic limit, so raise kn until it goes green. The cost is substeps — dt scales as 1/√kn.

Why X1 ≠ X2. A frictional floor can hold a horizontal load of up to μw·N, so the two side walls are free to differ by that much and still be in perfect static equilibrium — nothing drives them together. The Σ force residual row is the real equilibrium test: it goes to ~0% when the bed is static, whatever X1−X2 happens to be. To force X1 = X2 exactly, set μw = 0 (frictionless walls, frictional grains) — the standard setup for a clean stress measurement, and the default here.

Two of these are not supposed to be zero. The floor must carry the whole weight (bottom/W → 1), and a bed under gravity always pushes outward — Rankine gives side/W ≈ K·H/L with K ≈ 0.3–0.5, so K is the number to judge, not the raw force.

The real test of minimum wall force is Unload. It sets g = 0. A packing that merely settled goes slack and every wall drops to zero. A packing that was squeezed against the walls keeps pushing — that residue is locked-in stress, and it is the thing worth avoiding.
