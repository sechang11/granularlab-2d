Display

What everything means

Everything is SI inside (metres, kilograms, newtons). Only the display converts: grains in mm, the box in cm, forces in N or kN. The disks are unit-thickness, so a force here is really force per metre of depth.
Click anywhere outside this box, or press Esc, to close it.

Particles

count N
How many disks to create on the next Fill.
d
Mean grain diameter in mm. 30 mm is gravel; real bead experiments run 1–3 mm, and the control reaches down to 2.
±x
Dispersity. Diameters are drawn uniformly from d(1±x). At 0% every grain is identical and the packing crystallises readily; 10–20% suppresses that and is what most real powders look like.
ρ
Grain density, kg/m³. Applies to grains already on screen: their mass and inertia are re-derived at once.
next fill
N, d and ±x decide which grains exist, so they take effect when you next press Fill. Everything else is live.

Box & filling

X1 X2 Y1 Y2
Wall positions in cm. Type a number, drag the slider, or drag the wall itself in the view. Select a wall by clicking it, then nudge with the arrow keys (1 mm, or 10 mm with Shift).
target fill
What fraction of the box the grains should occupy when Size box computes a box for them, and when Fill enlarges a box too small to hold N.
a box too small for N
Is no longer refused. The box has the free parameter, so the box gives way: Fill enlarges it to the size that holds N at the target fill, and says so. A box that already holds N is left exactly where you put it.
Pluviation
Rains grains in from above with friction already active, so contacts lock on first touch and the bed arrests before it can rearrange — the loosest route. Measured φ ≈ 0.828, and raising μ opens it further (φ ≈ 0.822 at μ = 0.8). It needs free space above the bed, so Size box leaves headroom for it.
Pluviation, frictionless
The same deposition with μ forced to 0, so disks roll into the deepest well available; friction is restored once the bed is quiet. Measured φ ≈ 0.845, Z ≈ 4.3 — the densest route at realistic dispersity.
Grow in place
Seeds scattered at 35% radius, then inflated where they lie under gravity but without friction. No deposition front, so the packing is isotropic. Measured φ ≈ 0.836, Z ≈ 4.1.
Lattice release
A jittered triangular lattice released at once. Crystallises only when the grains are all one size: φ ≈ 0.869 at ±0% (the hexagonal limit is 0.9069), but ≈ 0.820 at ±15%, where dispersity frustrates the lattice and it becomes the loosest of the four.
deposition rate
How fast grains are released, per second of simulated time. Slower gives a looser bed.

Consolidating to σ3

⚖ Consolidate
Brings the left wall, the right wall and the lid to the same pressure, σ3, and the box as close to square as the bed will hold. All four walls move: the two side walls as a mirrored pair about the middle of the box, and the lid and the floor as another.
pressure
Force divided by the length of the wall it acts on. The side walls are as long as the box is tall (y2 − y1); the lid and the floor as long as it is wide (x2 − x1). With lengths in centimetres that is F / (L/100), in pascals. The disks are unit thickness, so a wall of length L metres presents L m² of face and this is a genuine pressure.
why pairs
A pair squeezes the bed from both ends at once. One wall pushing against a fixed one also shoves the whole bed: with the floor held still, the lid set the bed bouncing on the floor, its pressure swinging ±2 kPa either side of σ3, and the bed never went still.
level and difference
The walls are not steered one at a time. Closing both pairs together raises the pressure level; moving them against each other — sides in, lid out — changes the difference between the side walls and the lid. The bed answers those two separately, so each has its own loop and neither fights the other.
1 · touch
The lid comes down onto the bed.
2 · squaring
The lid rides the bed at a light load, 5% of σ3, while the side walls walk in, lifting the bed, or out, letting it slump. A bed only changes shape at its yield point, and it springs part of the way back once that push is let go, so squaring deliberately aims past square by the spring-back it expects.
3 · loading
Both pairs close until the pressure level reaches σ3.
4 · equalizing
The difference is let go: left, right and lid all to σ3. This is where the box springs back.
5 · squaring passes
If the box came out more than 0.4% from square, the walls push past square, let go and measure again — up to five times, and not after two passes in a row that did not help. Each pass learns how much of a push this bed keeps: a push the same way the bed last sprang keeps most of itself, a push against it first has to undo that spring.
6 · holding
Declared once the left wall, the right wall and the lid have each been within 1% of σ3 for a second and the bed is still. It is a hold, not a stop: the bed goes on relaxing, so the walls keep making micrometre corrections and the pressures stay at σ3 while you read and record them. Moving a wall by hand releases it; so do Fill, Tap, Unload and Clear.
when it will not square
The grains' own weight presses the side walls harder than the lid. When σ3 is small next to that weight, a square box cannot carry equal pressure on its sides and lid, and the walls open out until it can; the note then says so. Measured on 400 grains: square to 0.1% at 12 kPa, 2% out at 8 kPa, and 46% wider than tall at 5 kPa.
the floor
The one wall that cannot match. Σ vertical forces = 0 gives Fy1 = Fy2 + W, so the floor's pressure is the lid's plus the grains' weight spread over the box width — gravity, not the controller.
the side walls
With no wall friction (μw = 0, the default) Σ horizontal forces = 0 makes the left and right walls carry the same load. With wall friction the floor and the lid can hold part of a sideways difference, and the servo drives the average of the two side walls to σ3.
contact overlap
Pressure costs overlap. σ3 is always reached unless grains would overlap by more than 15% of a diameter, where a soft-contact model is no longer describing grains; then the load backs off and the note says what it reached. Past 8% the note warns. Raise kn for stiffer grains.
σ3 (kPa)
18 kPa is a typical laboratory confining stress and the default.
reading the result
The σ kPa column of the wall table is the check; when the box came out square the normal column agrees too. Forces there are averaged over about half a second, since within a single frame the bed sloshes. Every recorded row carries the pressures as well.

Top-left readouts

particles
How many disks currently exist.
t … s
Simulated time, in seconds — how long the grains have experienced, not how long you have been watching. It runs faster or slower than the clock depending on how much work each frame costs.
fps
Frames drawn per second. A display measure only; it says nothing about the physics.
packing φ
Packing fraction: the share of the measured region covered by disks, from 0 to 1. Loose is about 0.82, dense about 0.85, and the hexagonal limit for equal disks is 0.9069. It is estimated by throwing random points, so it carries roughly ±0.002 of sampling noise while it is being re-measured.
Z
Coordination number: the average number of touching neighbours per disk, counting disk-to-disk contacts only. In 2D the isostatic limits are Z = 4 for frictionless disks and Z = 3 for very frictional ones, so more friction gives a lower Z.
Z*
The same average with rattlers excluded. A rattler is a grain with fewer than two contacts — loose in a hole, carrying no load. Z* describes the load-bearing skeleton; Z describes every grain present.
KE
Total kinetic energy of the grains. It falls towards zero as the bed settles, and is how the app decides that it has.
max overlap
The deepest contact interpenetration, as a percentage of one grain diameter. Soft-disk DEM needs a little overlap to generate any force at all: under 1% is healthy, over about 5% means kn is too soft for the load and φ and Z stop being trustworthy.

The wall table

X1 X2 Y1 Y2
The four walls: left, right, floor, lid.
normal
Force pressing straight onto that wall.
shear
Force along the wall face — friction. Zero whenever μw = 0.
/ W
Divided by the total weight of the grains. A ratio, so it stays comparable across grain sizes and counts. The floor should read close to 1.000 — it carries everything. The lid should read 0.000 unless the packing is being pressed into its container.
weight W
Σmg over every grain: the yardstick the / W column divides by.
X1−X2 imbalance
How unequal the two side walls are. It need not be zero: a frictional floor can hold the difference, up to μw·N, which is the budget shown beside it. Set μw = 0 to force them exactly equal.
Σ force residual
Whether the packing as a whole is in equilibrium, in x and in y, as a percentage of W. This is the real test that a bed is static — not whether X1 equals X2. Near 0% is right.
K
Effective earth-pressure coefficient, side·L/(W·H): how hard the bed pushes sideways relative to its own weight. Jaky’s estimate is 1 − sin(atan μ), about 0.71 at μ = 0.3.

Contact model

kn
Normal contact stiffness, in N/m. Force = kn × overlap, so a stiffer setting means less interpenetration. It must grow with bed depth, because deeper beds press harder.
kt/kn
Tangential stiffness as a fraction of the normal one. Around 0.8 is typical.
ζ
Contact damping ratio: γ = 2ζ√(meffk). 0 is perfectly bouncy, 1 is critically damped.
b
Background drag on every grain, per second, unrelated to contact. It removes energy everywhere and settles a bed sooner.
μ, μw, μr
Friction between grains, between a grain and a wall, and rolling resistance. Perfect disks would roll forever without the last one.

Records

● Record
Appends one line to the records table at the foot of the page: the four wall positions, forces and pressures, exactly as they stand the instant you press it. The button flashes and a note appears in the corner of the view, so you do not have to open the table to know it worked.
the inputs block
Above each table. It is written out again only when one of the inputs has changed since the last record, and a change starts a new table underneath it — so every table is a run taken under one configuration, and the columns are never interrupted. The value that changed is highlighted; hover it to see what it was.
x₁ x₂ y₁ y₂
Where the four walls actually are, in cm — not where a slider has asked them to go. Mid-sweep those differ.
Fx1 … Fy2
The normal force on each of those walls, in newtons, time-averaged the same way the readouts are. Left, right, floor, lid.
px1 … py2
The pressure on each wall, in kPa: its force divided by its own length. The side walls are y2 − y1 long and the floor and the lid x2 − x1, both stored in centimetres, so this is F / (L/100). Worked out from the row's own positions and forces rather than stored, so records taken before these columns existed have them too. The floor's includes the weight of the grains.
⚠ a marked row
Recorded while the bed was still moving. Those are not equilibrium forces, and nothing downstream could tell from the numbers alone. Wait for the bed to settle, or press Tap and wait, before recording.
walls and display
Deliberately absent from the inputs block. The walls are the first four columns of every row already; the display choices — colour scheme, rotation marks, measurement region — are not physics, and a record should not be re-stamped because you turned a marker on.
Download CSV / TXT
The CSV keeps the table's structure, with a leading kind column so a spreadsheet can still separate the inputs lines from the readings. The TXT is the same content aligned to read as a notebook. Records are kept in this browser and survive a reload; Clear records asks first.

Worth knowing

next fill
N, d and ±x decide which grains exist, so they take effect when you next press Fill. Every other setting applies immediately to the grains already on screen.
Pause
Freezes the physics and the readouts, so φ stops re-sampling and stops twitching in its last digit. Fill, Tap and Unload resume it themselves — a command that means “make something happen” should not sit waiting for a Run you did not know was needed. Walls are the exception: they move by sweeping, which is physics, so a wall you move while paused waits for Run and says so.
workspaces A B C
Three independent benches. Each keeps its own settings, in this browser, and its own packing, for this session. Switching brings back that workspace's bed paused, so it cannot evolve while you read it; a workspace you have not filled yet is simply empty. Reset returns the one you are on to the defaults.
Fit view
Re-frames the camera on the box. The view never moves by itself, so dragging one wall can never appear to move another.
